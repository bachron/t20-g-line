**Created:** 25 Sep 2026
**Purpose:** compare the builds side by side on the three things that matter: **weight, comfort and cost.**

## 1. The answer first

| Cash out before reselling the G Line's stock parts | same as above | ~S$7,700-9,700 | ~S$7,000-8,200 |
| **Comfort** | Same contact points | Same contact points | Same contact points |
| Carrying the folded bike | **Lightest** | ~0.9-1.0 kg heavier | ~1.0-1.1 kg heavier |
| Hassle | Low: one frameset order, parts fit G Line spec | **High**: buy a whole bike, strip it, sell the leftovers | High, plus an eBay triangle with unconfirmed axle spec |

### Verdict

**Build A is the better build.** It is ~1 kg lighter for roughly the same money.

- Taking the midpoints, A costs ~S$650 more than the cheapest G Line route (B2) and saves ~1,000 g. **That's ~S$0.65 per gram.** Nothing else in the upgrade list comes close: pedals and saddle are ~S$3/g, the THM crank ~S$23/g. **The Ti main frame is the cheapest weight saving in the whole project.**
- B only wins on cost if the G Line's stock parts resell well (the ~S$800-1,500 assumption below). If they sell slowly or cheaply, B costs as much as A or more and is still a kilo heavier.
- **Comfort is a tie.** Both builds use the same saddle, grips, bar, tyres and wheels, and those set the ride feel, not the frame material. The one comfort difference you'll notice every day is **lifting and carrying the folded bike**, and A wins that by ~1 kg.
- **Where B is genuinely better:** a genuine Brompton main frame and hinge, with the fold refinement and resale value that brings. The HEXATI is a clone with 3D-printed hinges. If fold quality and resale matter more to you than 1 kg, B is a fair choice.

> ⚠️ `T20_Build_Plan.md` lists the Ti frame and rear triangle as **✅ Purchased**. If you've already paid Chris Yeo, the frameset is a sunk cost and Build A is far cheaper than B from here. This comparison assumes the frameset is not yet paid for.

## 2. Assumptions

**Used pricing:** used parts are estimated at **55-70% of new price**. This is a rule of thumb for Carousell; real prices vary.

**Always new (your rule):** bottom bracket, grips, brake and shift cables/housing.

**Also worth buying new (my suggestion, your call):**
- **Chain**: used chains are usually worn, and a worn chain wears the S$250 cassette and the carbon chainring. ~S$60 new.
- **Tyres and tubes**: cheap new, and the rubber ages even on unused tyres.
- **Carbon handlebar**: a used carbon bar can hide crash damage you can't see. With a child seat on the bike, new is the safer choice. It adds ~S$70-100.

The cost ranges below already treat the chain, tyres and tubes as new. The handlebar is priced as used.

**Weights** come from `T20_Build_Options.md`, using the current pick in each category. **Not included** (same as the tracker): child seats and mounts, lights, mudguards, kickstand.

**★ = new price is my estimate**; the tracker has no confirmed price. Check these before buying.

## 5. Build B: used Brompton G Line, main frame only kept

**How it works:** buy a used G Line (~**S$2,800-3,200** on Carousell; new is S$3,980-4,915). Keep the main frame, plus the hinge clamps, seatpost clamp, rear block, easy wheels and E-hook that come with it. Everything else is replaced by the shared parts list, and the stripped stock parts are sold.

**Two rear-end options:**

| Main frame | G Line steel, **2,290g** | G Line steel, **2,290g** |

| Frame hardware | **Comes with the bike** (~250-350g, S$0) | same |
| Resale of stock parts | **−S$800-1,500 ★** (wheels with Alfine hub, tyres, fork, steel triangle, stem, bar, brakes, crank, seatpost, saddle, pedals, rack) | same |
| **Total weight** | **9,434-9,886g** | **9,503-9,955g** |
| **Net cost** | **S$6,206-8,878** | **S$5,465-7,397** |

**Pros**
- Genuine Brompton main frame and hinge: proven fold, better resale.
- The frame hardware comes free with the bike.
- G Line stock brakes are **flat mount**, so the flat-vs-post question goes away.
- B2 is the cheapest route overall, **if** the stock parts resell.

**Cons**
- **~0.9-1.1 kg heavier.** The steel main frame alone is 939g over the Ti one, and no parts choice gets that back.
- A lot more work: find a good used bike, strip it, then sell a dozen parts on Carousell. The resale money arrives slowly and isn't guaranteed.
- A used frame has unknown history. Check the hinge and main tube for damage and corrosion.
- **Stock G Line is a hub-gear bike** (Shimano Alfine 8, 135mm QR rear). Going derailleur means the Ti triangle swap (B1/B2) or a TPW GRP-21B hanger. The 8.3kg reference build shows the conversion works.
- ⚠️ **Unconfirmed:** that a Ti stem made for the G Line pattern is available to buy. The HEXATI Ti stem should fit, since the T20 clones the G Line, but confirm it with Chris Yeo. B2's Titanium-DX triangle axle spec is also unconfirmed.

## 6. Comfort: why it's a tie

What you feel on the bike comes from, in order: **tyres → saddle → grips → bar position → frame.** Builds A and B share every one of the first four:

- **Continental Contact Urban 2.0"** tyres: high volume, the biggest comfort factor.
- **Tioga Spyder Stratum** saddle: suspended shell.
- **Ergon GP2 Evo** grips with bar ends: palm support and a second hand position.
- Same carbon bar, same G Line geometry.

Ti vs steel for the main frame makes little difference you can feel on a small-wheeled folder with 2.0" tyres. The comfort difference you *will* notice is **off the bike**: carrying the folded bike up stairs or onto a train. There, A is ~1 kg lighter.

## 7. Before deciding

1. **Is the T20 frameset already paid for?** If yes, the decision is made: Build A.
2. **Get the fork credit from Chris Yeo.** It lowers Build A by that amount.
3. **For Build B:** check Carousell asking prices for stripped G Line parts (Alfine wheels, fork, triangle) to firm up the S$800-1,500 resale estimate. It decides whether B is cheaper at all.
4. **For Build B:** confirm a G Line-pattern Ti stem you can buy.
5. **Either way:** start collecting the shared parts (section 3) used now. They fit both builds.
