### Verdict

**One of the T20 builds. Which one depends on the complete bike's real price.**

- **T20-A vs T20-B:** the frame is the same, so weight is the same. T20-A also comes with the frame hardware, headset, fork, hanger and thru-axle, and a new 105 groupset to sell. **At S$2,900, T20-A is ~S$1,300 cheaper** at the midpoints, once stock parts are sold. **At S$3,200, it's still ~S$1,000 cheaper**, if the parts resell. If you'd rather not sell a groupset on Carousell, T20-B is the simpler route for about the same cash out.
- **T20 vs G Line:** the T20 is ~1 kg lighter for about the same money or less. The Ti main frame alone saves 939g, and nothing else in the project saves weight that cheaply (~S$0.65/g).
- **Comfort is a tie.** Every build uses the same saddle, grips, bar, tyres and wheels, and those set the ride feel, not the frame material. The difference you'll feel is carrying the folded bike.
- **Where the G Line is better:** a genuine Brompton main frame and hinge, with its proven fold and resale value.

## 2. Assumptions

**Used pricing:** used parts are estimated at **55-70% of new price**. This is a rule of thumb for Carousell; real prices vary.

**Always new (your rule):** bottom bracket, grips, brake and shift cables/housing.

**Also worth buying new (my suggestion, your call):**
- **Chain**: used chains are usually worn, and a worn chain wears the S$250 cassette and the carbon chainring. ~S$60 new.
- **Tyres and tubes**: cheap new, and the rubber ages even on unused tyres.
- **Carbon handlebar**: a used carbon bar can hide crash damage you can't see. With a child seat on the bike, new is the safer choice. It adds ~S$70-100.

The cost ranges below already treat the chain, tyres and tubes as new. The handlebar is priced as used.

**Resale of stock parts** (T20-A and G Line builds) is my estimate. It's the least certain number here, and it decides whether those builds are cheaper.

**Weights** come from `T20_Build_Options.md`, using the current pick in each category. **Not included** (same as the tracker): child seats and mounts, lights, mudguards, kickstand.

**★ = new price is my estimate**; the tracker has no confirmed price. Check these before buying.

## 6. G Line build: used Brompton G Line, main frame only kept

**How it works:** buy a used G Line (~**S$2,800-3,200** on Carousell; new is S$3,980-4,915). Keep the main frame, plus the hinge clamps, seatpost clamp, rear block, easy wheels and E-hook that come with it. Everything else is replaced by the shared parts list, and the stripped stock parts are sold.

**Two rear-end options:**

| Part | **G1**: best weight | **G2**: best cost |
|---|---|---|
| Main frame | G Line steel, **2,290g** | G Line steel, **2,290g** |
| Rear triangle | tibicycles Ultra-Light Ti, **633g**, 142×12. S$1,080-1,770 new (rare used) | Titanium-DX Ti, **~800g** |
| Fork | Silverock carbon, 388g, S$180-230 used | Titanium-DX carbon, **290g** |
| Triangle + fork cost | ~S$1,260-2,000 | **~S$519** for the set (US$399) |
| Stem | Ti G Line-pattern stem, 450-560g, ~S$300-385 used / ~S$550 new ★ | same |
| Hanger + thru-axle | GRP-22B 54g + axle ~40g | same, ⚠️ once the Ti-DX axle spec is confirmed |
| Frame hardware | **Comes with the bike** (~250-350g, S$0) | same |
| Resale of stock parts | **−S$800-1,500 ★** (wheels with Alfine hub, tyres, fork, steel triangle, stem, bar, brakes, crank, seatpost, saddle, pedals, rack) | same |
| **Total weight** | **9,434-9,886g** | **9,503-9,955g** |
| **Net cost** | **S$6,206-8,878** | **S$5,465-7,397** |

**Pros**
- Genuine Brompton main frame and hinge: proven fold, better resale.
- The frame hardware comes free with the bike.
- G Line stock brakes are **flat mount**, so the flat-vs-post question goes away.
- G2 is the cheapest route overall, **if** the stock parts resell.

**Cons**
- **~0.9-1.1 kg heavier.** The steel main frame alone is 939g over the Ti one, and no parts choice gets that back.
- A lot more work: find a good used bike, strip it, then sell a dozen parts on Carousell. The resale money arrives slowly and isn't guaranteed.
- A used frame has unknown history. Check the hinge and main tube for damage and corrosion.
- **Stock G Line is a hub-gear bike** (Shimano Alfine 8, 135mm QR rear). Going derailleur means the Ti triangle swap (G1/G2) or a TPW GRP-21B hanger. The 8.3kg reference build shows the conversion works.
- ⚠️ **Unconfirmed:** that a Ti stem made for the G Line pattern is available to buy. The HEXATI Ti stem should fit, since the T20 clones the G Line, but confirm it with Chris Yeo. G2's Titanium-DX triangle axle spec is also unconfirmed.

## 7. Comfort: why it's a tie

What you feel on the bike comes from, in order: **tyres → saddle → grips → bar position → frame.** Every build shares the first four:

- **Continental Contact Urban 2.0"** tyres: high volume, the biggest comfort factor.
- **Tioga Spyder Stratum** saddle: suspended shell.
- **Ergon GP2 Evo** grips with bar ends: palm support and a second hand position.
- Same carbon bar, same G Line geometry.

Ti vs steel for the main frame makes little difference you can feel on a small-wheeled folder with 2.0" tyres. The comfort difference you *will* notice is **off the bike**: carrying the folded bike up stairs or onto a train. There, the T20 builds are ~1 kg lighter.

1. **Ask Chris Yeo the complete-bike price** (S$2,900 or S$3,200) and whether it can come with the **Ti stem** instead of carbon. This decides T20-A vs T20-B.

4. **For T20-A and the G Line:** check Carousell prices for a used 105 groupset and stripped G Line parts to firm up the resale estimates.
